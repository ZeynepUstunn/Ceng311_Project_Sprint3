7 Important Symptoms of Depression

Depression is not only a condition that affects mood but also a disease that can negatively affect physical health and daily life. Everyone experiences depression differently, but there are some common symptoms. Here are 7 important symptoms of depression:

1. Feeling Constantly Sad and Blue

The most common symptom of depression is feeling constantly sad, empty, or unhappy. This feeling can last for days or even weeks, and often it is difficult for the person to return to a normal mood.

2. Loss of Energy and Fatigue

Individuals with depression often feel tired of everything. Even the simplest daily tasks can seem extremely exhausting. This loss of energy can make a person feel physically and mentally drained.

3. Loss of Interest and Pleasure

The inability to enjoy activities that once brought joy is a symptom of depression. Hobbies, spending time with friends, or social events can lose their appeal due to depression.

4. Sleep Disorders

Depression can also affect sleep patterns. Some people may have trouble sleeping, while others may tend to sleep excessively. Sleep disorders can further worsen both the physical and emotional effects of depression.

5. Concentration Problems

Depression can lead to a lack of mental clarity. Individuals may have difficulty making decisions, focusing at work or school, which can negatively affect work efficiency and the completion of daily tasks.

6. Appetite Changes

Depression can lead to significant changes in appetite. Some individuals may eat excessively, while others may lose their appetite altogether. These changes can make a person feel worse both physically and emotionally.

7. Feeling Worthless and Guilty

Individuals with depression often feel worthless, inadequate, or guilty. These thoughts can undermine a person's self-confidence and lead to deeper depression.

Depression is a serious condition that can significantly impact a person's quality of life. However, early detection and seeking professional help can speed up the treatment process. If you notice these symptoms in yourself or others, seeking support from a specialist is important.

Remember, depression is a treatable condition. Don't hesitate to contact a psychologist to get help for yourself or your loved ones.
